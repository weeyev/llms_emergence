Selection rule: a paper is counted as selected when the final human adjudication retains at least one explicitly checked emergence claim and does not mark the paper disputed, removed, or rejected. “Selected” means retained for the final evidence set—not evidence of broad emergent intelligence.

Method

How the audit was conducted

  1. A broad corpus was assembled from emergence claims, related mechanisms, controls, counterclaims, and lineage papers.
  2. Papers were triaged by reading priority, then checked against original sources, later versions, code, data, and criticism where available.
  3. The strongest papers were scored on scaling, criticality, compression, novel bases, generalization, knowledge-in-mechanism, and capability boundary.
  4. In the 1st vetting, the initial Codex matrix was independently checked by Vihan and reconciled under stricter human decision rules.
  5. In the 2nd vetting, Codex ratings were preserved and later human confirmations or overrides were recorded separately.
Output

What the process produced

  • 1st vetting: an 18-paper mechanistic matrix, paper-level evidence notes, Vihan’s review, and a final human ranking.
  • Strongest 1st-vetting results: Which Attention Heads Matter?, grokking progress measures, multimodal function vectors, the any-order Markov construction, and linear spatial world models.
  • 2nd vetting: 11 sources inspected, 10 final verdicts, 6 papers retaining at least one checked claim, and 1 paper without a supplied human verdict.
  • Strongest 2nd-vetting results: foveal image sampling, free-recall memory strategies, and path integration/object-location binding.
  • Overall conclusion: the audit retains scoped evidence for emergent capabilities, but no paper establishes broad emergent intelligence.

Decision rules that changed scores

Compression and novel bases

  • Compression requires a genuinely reduced representation or effective description that preserves function and preferably improves efficiency or robustness. A few decoded variables or a sparse component list is not sufficient.
  • Novel bases requires a substantively different internal representational scheme. Structure directly encouraged by the architecture/objective, or produced by analyst-defined clustering, is not automatically emergent.

Generalization, scaling, and criticality

  • Generalization requires success beyond held-out examples or related tasks in the same family; broad claims need transfer to meaningfully different tasks or domains.
  • Scaling requires a scale-linked internal reorganization that helps explain a new capability, not performance improvement alone.
  • Criticality requires a transition tied to a changed internal organization or reduced description, not only an output threshold.
